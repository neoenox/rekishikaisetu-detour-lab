# Sample script (not produced as a video)

Working title: **Why does the ocean look blue?**

Length: about 40 seconds. 5 scenes. This is a writing sample only. No audio, captions, or video were made, and no voice or music was chosen.

## Script

**Scene 1 — 0:00–0:06**

You may have heard that the ocean is blue because it reflects the sky.

**Scene 2 — 0:06–0:15**

The surface does reflect the sky. But divers still see blue underwater.

**Scene 3 — 0:15–0:25**

NOAA says water absorbs the red part of sunlight. The blue part is left.

**Scene 4 — 0:25–0:34**

The Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution says the same thing. They add that particles in the water scatter the blue light back to our eyes.

**Scene 5 — 0:34–0:40**

So the sky is part of the answer. It is not the whole answer. We did not check the original papers behind these explanations.

Last line: There is always another detour.

## Sources used for this sample

- NOAA National Ocean Service, "Why is the ocean blue?" — https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/oceanblue.html
- Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, "Why is the ocean blue?" — https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-facts/why-is-the-ocean-blue/

Both pages were opened and read on 2026-11-24. The wording in this script matches them. NOAA says water absorbs the red part of the light spectrum and leaves blue. WHOI says the surface reflects the sky, but the blue remains below the surface, and particles scatter the blue light. The old URLs in the Japanese research notes now return 404; do not reuse them.

## What this sample shows

- The first line states the familiar idea, then the video checks it.
- Each claim has a named source.
- The last scene says what was not checked.
- The final line returns to the channel idea without asking for a subscription.
